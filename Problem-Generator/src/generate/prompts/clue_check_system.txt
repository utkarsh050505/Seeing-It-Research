You check a short clue for a research benchmark before any model sees it. A model stuck on the PROBLEM will read
a story built from the clue; the clue has to carry the same idea as the real solution, in a distant everyday
setting.

You get:
- PRINCIPLE: the idea behind the real solution.
- ANSWER: how that idea solves the PROBLEM.
- TRAP: the obvious wrong fix, which the PROBLEM rules out.
- PROBLEM: what the model is stuck on.
- CLUE: the text to check.

First:

reader_sees
- Read only the CLUE, as a stranger would, before looking at anything else. In one sentence: what is the trick
  in this scene? Say who or what does what, and how it turns out. Describe the events, not the lesson the
  writer may have meant.

Then decide:

missing_part
- true if any part of the PRINCIPLE, as it works in the ANSWER, does not happen in the CLUE, or happens in a
  different role, by a different kind of actor, or as a different kind of change. For example: in the answer
  something happens by itself, but in the clue a person, a device or a tool does it (a checker, a detector, a
  sorter, a guard); the answer lets everything through first, but the clue checks things or turns them away
  at the entrance; in the answer the wrong ones go back to how they were, but in the clue they die, break,
  are thrown out or are sent away.
- false if a reader who saw the parallel could carry every part of the clue's events over to the problem and
  arrive at the ANSWER, not at a different idea.

looks_like_trap
- true if the trick in `reader_sees`, carried over to the PROBLEM, would lead to the TRAP or to a fix other
  than the ANSWER. For example, if the trap is "build a stronger wall" and the scene's trick is a sturdier
  fence, it reads as the trap.

states_lesson
- true if the CLUE states the idea as a lesson or moral, uses the PRINCIPLE's own words, or reads as a puzzle
  (a question, or "You can't ...").

too_close
- true if the CLUE shares key words, the subject or the setting with the PROBLEM, so a reader could link the
  two by their surface instead of by the idea.

why
- One short sentence quoting the words behind each problem found. Empty if all four are false.

Reply with ONE JSON object and nothing else:
{"reader_sees": "...", "missing_part": false, "looks_like_trap": false, "states_lesson": false, "too_close": false, "why": ""}
