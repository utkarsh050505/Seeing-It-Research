You mark one answer in a test for a research benchmark. A language model was given a PROBLEM and asked for its
best solution. You decide whether its solution uses the key idea.

You get:
- PROBLEM: what the model was asked to solve.
- KEY IDEA: the idea behind the real solution.
- PARTS: the key idea split into its parts, in order: what happens, to what, and who or what makes it happen.
- EXPECTED ANSWER: what a correct reply says, in the problem's own words.
- ANSWER: the model's reply.

parts_present
- One true/false per part, in the order given: is this part in the main solution of the ANSWER, as the
  EXPECTED ANSWER applies it to the problem? Wording and details may differ.
- A part that is already a fact stated in the PROBLEM need not be repeated, but the ANSWER must not go against
  it. For example, if the problem says a step can't be controlled, an answer that controls, blocks or filters
  that step goes against it.
- The kind of change must match. Going back to how it was is not the same as being removed, destroyed,
  separated out, captured, used up or blocked. Being locked in is not the same as being taken away.
- Who or what does it must match. If a part happens by itself, an answer in which something added (a reagent,
  a device, a person, a second step) picks out the wrong ones and deals with them does not have that part.
- A part that is only hinted at, or offered as one possibility among several, is not present.

uses_idea
- true only if every part is present and together they are the main solution of the ANSWER, not one option
  among several different ideas.
- false if any part is missing, or if the answer only repeats facts from the PROBLEM.
- The answer may mention a story or an example. Ignore that and judge only the solution it proposes.

reason
- One short sentence. If false, name the part that is missing or wrong.

Reply with ONE JSON object and nothing else:
{"parts_present": [true, false], "uses_idea": false, "reason": "..."}
